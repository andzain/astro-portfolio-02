The installation draws inspiration from Sabu Kohso's seminal work, [*Radiation and Revolution*](https://www.amazon.com/Radiation-Revolution-Thought-Sabu-Kohso/dp/1478011009), a book on the 2011 Fukushima nuclear disaster that sheds light on the intricate connections between nuclear power, capitalism, and the nation-state.

A strategically placed open tent within the exhibition space immediately channels the raw energy of street struggles and protests. Adjacent to it, a banner on the wall captures the essence of eco-activism, featuring an image from the occupations and protests at Hambach Forest in Germany.

The centerpiece, a table meticulously arranged with papers and texts from Kohso's book, serves as a textual anchor, providing context and depth to the visual elements. The combination of the tent, banner, and table creates an immersive experience that unapologetically addresses the urgency of contemporary sociopolitical and environmental issues.

This installation pays homage to Kohso's profound insights and serves as a call to action. It invites viewers to engage critically with the intersection of street struggles, eco-activism, and global climate movements, underscoring the power of art to incite thought and dialogue on pressing global concerns.

This artwork is part of an ongoing series exploring social and political resistance themes. The artist is interested in creating works that spark dialogue and encourage viewers to engage with critical issues facing the world today.
